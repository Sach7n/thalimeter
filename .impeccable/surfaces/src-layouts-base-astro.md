Scope: whole site (all calculators and answer pages). Mode: Operate for calculators, Read for answer pages.
Audience/job: home cooks from Google, phone in hand, needing "how much rice for N people" in cups and grams.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Portion planning as a coding system, like the painted codes on Mumbai tiffin lids: every headcount and answer gets a painted lid mark you can read at arm's length. Refuses the category default of cream paper, serif display, emoji icon cards and a terracotta accent (which the old site was).

OWN-WORLD: Cool steel ground (pale brushed-steel grey) with white working plates, ink near-black. Four painted code colours, each owning one tool: vermilion = rice, ultramarine = water, chrome yellow = pasta, signal green = meat. Big Shoulders (sign-painter condensed) for numbers and headings; Public Sans (plain, legible US-government workhorse) for every word you read. Hairline steel rims, round-capped painted strokes, no shadows beyond one soft offset on the calculator plate.

STORY: Visitor sees the answer for their headcount in the first screen in cups and grams, adjusts guests in the calculator, trusts it because the assumptions link is beside it, and moves to the next headcount by its lid.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Ink header with the lid logo and tool nav, each tool tab underlined in its code colour. H1 question, then the answer plate in large condensed numerals (cups · grams, side and main) beside a painted lid carrying the headcount. Tool pages: the calculator plate starts within the desktop fold. Answer pages (rice/pasta/meat for N): the answer plate, snippet paragraph and table lead and the calculator follows them (adaptation, cited: the owner asked for snippet-friendly answers near the top, PRODUCT principle 1). On phones the answer plate is in view; the calculator follows.
